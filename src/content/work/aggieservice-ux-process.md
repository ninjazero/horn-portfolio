challenge: AggieService helps UC Davis employees and departmental HR staff with HR questions, forms, and case management. But the service team kept seeing the same friction. People struggled to understand what AggieService could help with, forms were hard to complete accurately, and requesters couldn't see where their cases stood or why they were delayed. The goal wasn't to redesign a screen. It was to understand how employees, HR specialists, and departmental staff actually worked, find where confusion and rework happened, and give the service team a defensible, user-centered plan.
challengePoints:
  - Forms needed clearer instructions, labels, and workflows. Errors and workarounds were delaying service.
  - The portal didn't clearly explain what AggieService was or which services it offered.
  - Case delays were hard to understand, because status and explanations weren't visible to users.
challengeQuote: How might we inform, educate, and help UC Davis employees with the resolution of HR processes?

roleIntro: As a UX Designer with UC Davis IET Web Development, I worked with stakeholders from HR Shared Services and IET Enterprise Platforms on a structured, year-long engagement. I guided the team from defining the problem through research, synthesis, ideation, prototyping, usability testing, and a roadmap. Rather than starting from a predetermined redesign, the process gave the client team room to state its goals, challenge assumptions, and judge ideas against evidence from real users.

approach: We moved deliberately from shared understanding to evidence, and from evidence to testable ideas. Each phase narrowed the problem and made the next decision more concrete.
flow:
  label: Engagement phases
  steps:
    - title: Align stakeholders and frame the problem
      text: A UX Canvas mapped users, value, current solutions, channels, resources, and known challenges, and the team agreed on what AggieService is and who it serves.
    - title: Gather user evidence
      text: Reviewed existing satisfaction data, ran a new UX survey built in Qualtrics, and interviewed subject experts.
    - title: Synthesize and prioritize
      text: Grouped findings into themes and turned insights into "How might we…" questions.
    - title: Ideate and choose directions
      text: Brainstormed, voted, and scored ideas on excitement, novelty, and practicality. Two went forward, a more conversational form and a clearer portal.
    - title: Prototype and test
      text: Tested an interactive Leave of Absence form, then an improved portal with a Student Hire/Recruitment form for experienced HR users.
    - title: Deliver a roadmap
      text: Compiled findings, prototypes, and test observations into a phased plan for next steps.
  caption: Phases as documented in the final presentation (July 1, 2022).

highlights:
  heading: What the research found
  intro: Survey data, interviews, and existing satisfaction data pointed to the same recurring needs. The theme labels are my grouping; the findings come from the final presentation.
  items:
    - title: Communication and clarity
      text: Users often arrived with little idea what AggieService could help with, and case submitters were confused about a case's status and the steps to resolve it.
    - title: Consistency
      text: Every department's HR managed employee interactions differently. Users asked for checklists and resources to complete forms quickly and accurately.
    - title: Speed
      text: Users were most satisfied when they got answers by email or the website with little back-and-forth, and wanted to get the right information into a form the first time.
    - title: Confidence
      text: Users wanted to know they were in the right place, taking the right actions, and that someone on the other end was following up.

decisionsIntro: These summarize choices documented in the final presentation. They were made with the team, and the tradeoffs are my own reflection.
decisions:
  - title: Diagnose the service before redesigning it
    problem: Stakeholders saw form errors, portal confusion, and poor visibility into delays, but those symptoms could have many causes.
    alternative: Jumping straight into fixing forms and the portal.
    why: The team needed to separate assumptions from real user needs and agree on what improvement should accomplish.
    tradeoff: Research took time before any visible interface changes.
    source: deck
  - title: Test a conversational form, not just clearer labels
    problem: Dense, unfamiliar HR forms left users unsure what information was needed.
    why: A guided Leave of Absence flow could add contextual help, conditional questions, and up-front guidance on what to prepare.
    findings: The conversational form consistently got positive feedback, and users liked the question-mark help and calendar date pickers. They also asked to save progress, see auto-filled values, add comments, and know what happens after submitting.
    tradeoff: Richer workflows and integrations need more technical investigation than copy edits.
    source: deck
  - title: Test the portal inside a realistic advanced workflow
    problem: A portal mockup on its own might not expose the complexity frequent HR users deal with.
    why: After the first prototype, the team decided the portal was part of the journey to a form, so it would be tested along the way.
    findings: The second round asked an HR professional to hire three student workers, testing navigation and an advanced Student Hire/Recruitment form together.
    tradeoff: The portal got less isolated testing, in exchange for a more realistic end-to-end test.
    source: deck
